The Case Study

A quarterly nurse rostering tool for a synthetic 30-nurse Danish hospital ward, built as a mixed-integer linear program (MILP) grounded in real Danish labour law wherever possible. This page explains what the model actually decides, what every constraint is based on, and where the numbers are assumptions rather than sourced facts.

What the model decides

Every quarter, the ward needs a full shift roster: which of the 30 nurses works which shift, on which day, for 13 weeks. The model treats this as an optimisation problem — a real, well-established one in operations research called the nurse scheduling problem (NSP), first formalised in 1976 and known to be NP-hard. The model here uses the same approach as the two original 1976 papers on the topic: exact mathematical programming, solved with the open-source HiGHS solver via Julia's JuMP.

Hard constraints Sourced

These are non-negotiable rules the model can never violate. Wherever possible they're pulled directly from the real Danish overenskomst (OK-24, the collective agreement between Dansk Sygeplejeråd and Danske Regioner).

  • 11-hour rest rule between shifts, with an 8-hour emergency floor per the agreement's own wording. Applied here as three forbidden shift-adjacency pairs, derived directly from the shift clock times: Evening→Day, Night→Day, and Night→Evening.
  • 48-hour weekly cap, including any overtime, per the Working Time Directive as referenced in the agreement.
  • 37-hour/week average (scaled by each nurse's contract %), enforced as a ±15% band over the 13-week quarter rather than an exact target, since exact equality is generally infeasible against integer 8-hour shifts.
  • Approved vacation is a hard block — a nurse cannot be scheduled on a day they're marked as on leave.
  • Night eligibility — roughly a third of nurses don't participate in irregular-hour work at all, per DSR/Danske Regioner commentary on the agreement; those nurses are excluded from night shifts entirely.

Cost and wages Sourced

Every nurse has their own hourly wage, driven by role and years of experience — not a single flat rate per role. Nurses wages follow real DSR grundløn-by-anciennitet løntrin steps (25,382.67 kr./month newly graduated, rising to 30,738.99 kr./month at 10 years, extrapolated beyond that on the same slope). Healtcare Assistant (hereafter reffered to as "HA") wages are pinned to the same curve shape at a fixed ratio (78%), since no equivalent tillæg-free anciennitet table could be found for that role — mixing bases from different sources was tested and rejected after it produced an unrealistic result where HA pay could overtake nurses pay at high seniority.

Shift premiums (evening 32%, night 37.5%, weekend 47%, public holiday 50%) come directly from the OK-24 agreement text. Public holidays used in cost calculations are the real, verified 2025 Danish dates — including confirming that Store Bededag was permanently abolished from 2024 onward, so it's correctly excluded rather than assumed.

Known assumptions and gaps Assumption

Not everything here is sourced, and it's worth being precise about where judgement calls were made instead:

13-week quarters, not the real 14-week default. The overenskomst's actual default reference period ("normperiode") is 14 weeks if no local agreement is reached. This project deliberately uses 13-week calendar-aligned quarters instead, for cleaner "one roster per quarter" portfolio framing.
Nurse headcount runs close to its coverage limit. The nurse coverage rule demands more nurse-hours per week than the 24 nurses' contracted hours alone can supply. A ±15% overtime tolerance band is what makes the model feasible. Anything below approximately 12% tolerance is mathematically infeasible regardless of solver settings.
No maximum-consecutive-working-days constraint. This is a standard hard constraint in the canonical nurse scheduling problem definition that isn't currently implemented here — a real, acknowledged gap rather than a deliberate design choice.
Coverage targets are static, not patient-intensity-driven. A 2023 EU Commission-funded project at an actual Danish hospital (Regionshospitalet Gødstrup) found that real evidence-based staffing should flex with actual patient acuity, not a fixed daily headcount. This model uses fixed targets (8/5/3), closer to the "traditional" method that report explicitly critiques than to the evidence-based alternative it recommends.

What this model is not (yet)

The "Staffing Risk — Lookahead" panel on the results page currently shows illustrative, not real, numbers. A genuine forecast-driven sick-leave simulation layer — the piece that would make this a true simulation-optimisation tool rather than a static rostering MILP — is scoped but not yet built. This page exists specifically so that distinction is never left implicit.
