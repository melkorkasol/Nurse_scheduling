Nurse Population

All 30 nurses are generated once and reused across every quarter — as one can assume that deparment's staff doesn't turn over every 13 weeks. What does change per quarter is each nurse's vacation pattern, since different times of year fall in different Danish holiday seasons (see the reasoning below).

How this population was generated

Every distribution below is either grounded in a real source or clearly flagged as a modeling assumption — that distinction matters more than the specific numbers.

Role split (80% Nurses / 20% HA) Sourced
This matches the evidence-based recommendation confirmed in a 2023 EU-funded Danish hospital study (Regionshospitalet Gødstrup, Central Denmark Region): at least 80% nurses to 20% healthcare assistants, citing Griffiths (2019) — above a certain threshold, higher shares of healthcare assistants have measurably harmful effects on patient care.
Contract % distribution (100%: 45%, 80%: 20%, 70%: 15%, 50%: 15%, 30%: 5%) Assumption
Informed by DSR/Danske Regioner reporting that roughly half of nurses work part-time, but the specific bracket weights are a modeling choice.
Night eligibility, conditioned on contract % Assumption
Probability of being night-eligible ranges from 80% (full-time) down to 15% (30% contract), plus a +10pp bump for HA. The overall ~1/3-non-participation figure is sourced from OK-24 commentary; the contract-based gradient and role bump are modeling choices layered on top of that real anchor.
Seniority (years of experience) Assumption
Drawn from a Beta(2,5) distribution scaled to 0–25 years — right-skewed toward early/mid-career, reflecting a typical ward's age structure. Not sourced to a specific dataset.
Vacation clustering, per quarter Partially sourced
Each quarter has its own seasonal "boost window" where vacation-day sampling probability is multiplied up: winter (vinterferie, ISO week 7), spring (real 2025 Easter dates, plus a pre-summer ramp-up), summer (peak sommerferie plus efterårsferie), and autumn/winter (Christmas, using verified 2025 dates). The holiday dates are real and confirmed; the specific boost multipliers (2–4x) are modeling assumptions.
